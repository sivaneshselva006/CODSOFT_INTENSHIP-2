01 / UNDERSTAND THE THREAT

What is phishing?

Phishing is a type of social engineering in which attackers impersonate trusted people or organizations to trick users into revealing information, opening harmful attachments, or visiting fraudulent websites.

✉

Fake messages

Emails or texts that imitate banks, delivery services, colleges, or popular platforms.

↗

Fraudulent links

Links that lead to look-alike websites designed to collect login or payment details.

!

Urgent pressure

Messages that create fear or excitement to make you act before checking the facts.

02 / SPOT THE WARNING SIGNS

Learn to recognize a suspicious message

These are fictional examples for education. Do not click links or provide information in response to unexpected messages.

SAMPLE EMAIL — NOT REAL

From: “Account Support” <security@example.invalid>

Subject: URGENT! Your account will close today

Dear user,

We detected a problem with your account. Verify your password immediately or your account will be suspended.

[Suspicious verification link omitted]

Thank you,
Account Support

Red flags to notice

01
Generic greeting

“Dear user” instead of your expected name or normal greeting.

02
Urgency or threats

Pressure to act immediately or risk losing access.

03
Unexpected credential request

Legitimate services should not ask you to send your password by email.

04
Unverified sender or link

Check the full sender address and domain independently.

LOOK-ALIKE WEBSITE WARNING

Check the address before signing in

Attackers may copy the appearance of a real login page. A padlock alone does not prove a site is trustworthy. Navigate to the service using its official app or a known address.

03 / BUILD SAFE HABITS

Protect yourself in a few simple steps

01

Verify independently

Contact the organization through its official website, app, or known phone number.

02

Inspect links

Check the real domain carefully. When unsure, do not open the link.

03

Use multi-factor authentication

Add a second sign-in factor and never approve a prompt you did not initiate.

04

Keep software updated

Install security updates and use reputable security tools.

05

Report suspicious activity

Use your email provider’s phishing-report option or notify your IT/security team.

06

Act quickly if caught

Change exposed passwords through the official site and contact your bank or IT team if needed.
